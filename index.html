How I design

Road geometric design first, delivered through BIM

Road and highway designs developed in accordance with the Saudi Road Design Manual (SRDM) and relevant Ministry of Transport and Logistic Services (MOTLS) requirements, with international best practice where applicable.

I am not only a BIM professional. I am a Road Geometric Design Engineer with practical experience in highway and infrastructure design, and the same engineering decisions sit behind every model I deliver.

Ten decisions behind every road design

Design speed and road classification

Road class, terrain, traffic and context set the design speed. Every later limit follows from that choice: minimum radius, maximum grade, sight distance, and lane and shoulder width.

Horizontal and vertical alignment design

Tangents, transition and circular curves, grades and vertical curves are developed together, so the road fits the corridor and its constraints and meets the limits for its design speed.

Sight distance requirements

Stopping sight distance is checked along horizontal and vertical curves, with decision and passing distance where the road class needs them, so what a driver can see matches the speed.

Superelevation design

Superelevation rates and runoff lengths are set for each curve to balance speed, comfort and drainage, with curve widening where required, and carried into the corridor model.

Cross-section development

Typical sections for lanes, shoulders, medians, drains and side slopes by road class, then adjusted along the road for cut, fill, structures and urban approaches.

Earthwork optimization

The grade line and sections are adjusted to balance cut and fill, shorten haul and limit embankment height, with quantities checked from the model at each design stage.

Drainage integration

Crossfall, longitudinal grades, side drains and culvert locations are considered while the geometry is set, so water leaves the road safely and drainage does not force late changes.

Road safety principles

Consistent speed along the route, forgiving side slopes and clear zones, safe junction and roundabout geometry, and signs and markings planned together with the alignment.

Constructability and maintenance

Practical widths, slopes and details that contractors can build and owners can maintain, checked against site constraints, existing services and construction phasing.

BIM-integrated design workflows

Civil 3D alignments, profiles, assemblies and corridors drive drawings and quantities, and the same model carries information into ISO 19650 deliverables, IFC and COBie.
